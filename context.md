# ELIZA Project – Intelligent Business Chatbot

**Project Name:** ELIZA  
**Type:** Academic/Professional Project (Epitech)  
**Objective:** Develop an intelligent chatbot for business use, focusing on identifying real needs, ethical considerations, and creating a functional, user-friendly product with measurable value.

---

## **1. Project Overview**

- **Background:**
  - ELIZA, one of the earliest chatbots, was developed in 1966 by Joseph Weizenbaum at MIT.
  - Modern chatbots have evolved from scripted tools to multimodal platforms driven by LLMs (e.g., GPT, Llama, Gemini, Claude).
  - AI and conversational agents are transforming business operations through contextual recognition and task automation.
- **Core Challenge:**  
Design and implement an intelligent chatbot that:
  - Serves a real business purpose.
  - Is valuable enough for a company to use or purchase.
  - Improves or streamlines business processes.

## **3. Ethics**

### **3.1 ELIZA Effect**

- **Definition:**  
The tendency to project human traits (e.g., understanding, feelings) onto simple chatbots, blurring the line between tool, companion, and mirror.
- **Awareness:**  
Recognize this effect and mitigate misplaced trust or emotional attachment.

### **3.2 Ethical Impact Assessment**

- **Objective:**  
Identify risks and ensure responsible use of technology.
- **Considerations:**
  - **Privacy:** Data collection and usage policies.
  - **Bias:** Avoid reinforcing stereotypes or discriminatory behavior.
  - **Transparency:** Clearly communicate the chatbot’s limitations.
  - **Accountability:** Define responsibility for chatbot actions/decisions.
- **Action Items:**
  - Conduct a risk assessment (e.g., data leaks, misuse).
  - Implement safeguards (e.g., data encryption, user consent protocols).
  - Regularly review and update ethical guidelines.

### **3.3 Embedding Ethics in Design**

- **Principles:**
  - **Fairness:** Ensure equitable treatment for all users.
  - **Respect:** Prioritize user dignity and autonomy.
  - **Safety:** Protect users from harm (e.g., misinformation, exploitation).
- **Implementation:**
  - Use ethical AI frameworks (e.g., EU AI Act, IEEE Ethics Guidelines).
  - Involve stakeholders (e.g., users, ethicists) in design reviews.
